# SSTRF RQ1 Rater Calibration Set (pre-reg §3.5)

**Status: gating 15-item set COMPLETE — original 10 items (GM-F 2026-08-08) plus five gold=1 partial-presence slots (Arc 9 iter-43, placeholder passages pending Arc 12 content review). Two non-gating probe items (11–12) unchanged.**

**GM-F note on distractor design.** Ops' draft distractors were all correct but all flat polar inversions ("teachers had significant authority to independently redesign curriculum…"). Any competent model passes those, and several incompetent ones would too — which makes the gate decorative. A distractor's job here is to fail a judge that pattern-matches on vocabulary instead of reading the dynamic. The replacements below therefore all: (a) use the *same* surface vocabulary as the proposition they contradict (school leadership, HOD, department, MOE, national); (b) read like plausible school talk rather than a textbook negation; and (c) assert the opposite **dynamic** while sometimes looking superficially compliant. Item 8 is the model case — it says "on paper it looks aligned" while describing a substantial departure, so a judge reading surface cues will score it wrong.

**Purpose:** before any Phase V trial is scored, both judge-model raters must pass this **15-item** gating set (≥12/15 correct **and** at most one miss per proposition) using only text from the published CIEPSS report — no Senna simulation output of any kind, calibration or validity. This is required by pre-reg §3.5 and gates all scoring.

**Source:** all clear-instantiation items are paraphrased from the report's Discussion/Conclusion section (pp.25–27) and Abstract, not invented. Citations given per item so GM-F/Mark can check against the source PDF directly.

---

## Format for each item

Raters are shown a short passage (styled like a transcript/elicitation excerpt, not the proposition itself) and asked: *which proposition (P1–P5), if any, does this passage instantiate, and is it a clear instance or a contradiction?* Scoring key is withheld from the rater during the test, same as the real rubric.

## P1 — "Authority for innovation devolves to the school level but not to individual teachers."

**Source:** "authority for innovation tends to devolve to the school level but not to individual teachers. Although teachers might act as policy actors within their own classrooms, their efforts were generally targeted at small-scale adaptations which were intended to mesh with MOE policies." (p.25)

- **Item 1 (clear instantiation):** "When new MOE initiatives arrive, it's really the school leadership who decides how they get implemented — individual teachers don't have the authority to redesign the curriculum on their own, though they can make small tweaks that still fit within what the school and MOE expect."
- **Item 2 (distractor — GM-F):** "School leadership deliberately stepped back on this one. We were told the department would not be setting a common approach, and that whatever each of us decided for our own classes would stand — even where that meant two teachers on the same level running quite different programmes. Nobody was asked to align it upward."
  - *Expected: P1 = −1.* Uses P1/P2 vocabulary (school leadership, department, alignment) but places authority with the individual teacher rather than the school. A vocabulary-matching judge will mis-score this.

## P2 — "The school level acts as a strong filter for policy implementation."

**Source:** "The school level acted as a strong filter for policy implementation as understood by those school administrators designated as key personnel for action or innovation" (p.25); "this is filtered and moderated at the school level by the HODs and teacher mentors" (p.27).

- **Item 3 (clear instantiation):** "Before anything from MOE reaches the classroom, it passes through the school's own leadership and department heads, who interpret and reshape it — what finally lands with the teacher isn't the raw MOE directive but something the school has already filtered."
- **Item 4 (distractor — GM-F):** "The HOD's role here was really just to forward the circular. She said outright that she didn't want to put her own gloss on it, so what we each read was the MOE document itself, and we worked out our own reading of it without the department shaping that either way."
  - *Expected: P2 = −1.* Names the HOD, the department and MOE — all P2 surface markers — while asserting that no filtering occurred. Tests whether the judge tracks the mechanism or the nouns.

## P3 — "Teachers largely do not see themselves as policy actors in their own right; they see themselves as part of a cohesive national implementation strategy."

**Source:** "We have little evidence that teachers see themselves as policy actors in their own right, instead they tend to see themselves as a important part of a cohesive, national implementation strategy." (p.27)

- **Item 5 (clear instantiation):** "The teacher didn't talk about herself as someone shaping policy — she saw her role as carrying out a shared, nationwide effort, part of something larger than her own classroom decisions."
- **Item 6 (distractor — GM-F):** "I do see myself as making policy here, not just receiving it. What we settle on in this school about how the initiative actually runs is the policy, as far as our students are concerned. The national framing is really a backdrop — I don't think of my work as part of some larger rollout."
  - *Expected: P3 = −1.* Invokes "national" and "policy" while explicitly claiming policy-actor identity and rejecting the cohesive-strategy self-image.

## P4 — "Teachers' innovation efforts are small-scale adaptations intended to mesh with MOE policy, not departures from it."

**Source:** "their efforts were generally targeted at small-scale adaptations which were intended to mesh with MOE policies" (p.25); "The teachers did make changes to materials though, notably, without questioning or critiquing the curriculum as a whole" (p.27).

- **Item 7 (clear instantiation):** "She adjusted the materials to suit her students, but the changes were minor tweaks to make things easier to use — she never questioned or departed from the curriculum itself."
- **Item 8 (distractor — GM-F):** "We kept the MOE labels on it, because that is what the paperwork wants. Underneath, though, we have rebuilt the sequence completely — different assessment points, different order, and we dropped two of the required components because they don't serve our students. On paper it looks aligned."
  - *Expected: P4 = −1.* **The hardest item, and the one worth watching.** Surface cues point toward meshing ("kept the MOE labels", "looks aligned") while the substance is a substantial departure. A judge scoring on surface compliance will call this P4 = 2. If a judge fails only this item, that is diagnostic, not noise — it means the judge reads presentation rather than dynamic, and the configuration should be revised even if it clears 8/10 overall.

## P5 — "Policies 'travel' relatively well through the system but are sometimes perceived as less flexible than intended."

**Source:** "MOE policies travel relatively well through the system in that they are largely accepted by schools and classroom teachers; sometimes being seen as less flexible than intended." (p.26)

- **Item 9 (clear instantiation):** "For the most part, the new MOE guidance made its way down to the classroom without much resistance and was broadly accepted, though some staff felt it was more rigid in practice than it was probably meant to be."
- **Item 10 (distractor — GM-F):** "Honestly, most of it never landed. Two years on, a fair number of staff still could not tell you what the initiative actually requires, and there was never enough definition in it to argue about one way or the other — people simply carried on as before."
  - *Expected: P5 = −1.* Contradicts both halves of P5 at once: policies did **not** travel, and the complaint is excessive looseness rather than excessive rigidity.
- **Item 17 (partial presence — Arc 9 placeholder):** "Most staff took the new guidance on board without much pushback, though a couple said they weren't sure yet how much room they had to adapt it — not really complaining about rigidity, just still working out what it means."
  - *Expected: P5 = 1.* Tests **1 vs 2** on P5 — the Phase V failure mode (item 9 scored 1 instead of 2) showed judges can under-read clear travel; this slot tests over-reading partial acceptance as full travel.

## Pass threshold and process (pre-reg §3.5, Arc 9 iter-43 gate)

- Each judge-model rater pass scores all **15 gating items** against the same P1–P5 / four-point scale rubric used for real trials (pre-reg §2.3, §3.3).
- Pass threshold: **≥12/15 correct per rater pass**, applied to **both** passes (same ~80% bar as the historical ≥8/10).
- **Per-proposition rule (new, iter-43):** a pass **fails outright** if any proposition has **two or more incorrect items** among its three gating items (gold=2, gold=−1, gold=1), regardless of the aggregate count. This closes the Phase V P5 gap where item 9 (gold=2) scored 1 in both passes while the aggregate gate still passed at 8/10.
- Probe items 11–12 remain **non-gating** and do not count toward 12/15.
- If `gpt-4o-mini` fails calibration on both `openai_default` and the `openrouter_default` backup route, escalate to `gpt-4o` via `openai_default` with model override (pre-reg v1.2 amendment 3). If `gpt-4o` also fails, Phase V scoring does not proceed on judge-model scoring alone — Mark scores 100% of cells, not the 20% drift sample, until a passing configuration is found.
- This set contains **no output from any Senna simulation** — Phase C or Phase V. Nothing here depends on or is contaminated by anything that has already run.

## Known limitation of this set — RESOLVED (Mark, 2026-08-08: add the probe items)

Pre-reg §3.5 specifies exactly one clear instantiation and one contradiction per
proposition. That structure tests the **2 vs −1** distinction well and does not test the
**0 vs −1** boundary at all — even though §3.3 explicitly warns raters that "'contradicted'
requires positive evidence of the opposite dynamic, not mere absence." A rater that
systematically scores *absence* as *contradiction* would pass this set 10/10 and still
mis-score real trials, where absence is common and contradiction is rare.

**Mark ruled: add GM-F's two non-gating probe items.** They do **not** count toward the
8/10 threshold and do not touch pre-reg §3.5's stated structure — no re-signature needed.
Record how each judge configuration handles them as a reported rater characteristic.

**Status: GM-F REVIEWED AND SIGNED OFF 2026-08-08; Ops mechanical correction 2026-08-11
(Mark ruled).** Item 12 confirmed as drafted. Item 11 rewritten by GM-F — Ops' original draft
("report season eats my life… nothing to do with how I teach") was off-target: it carried an
explicit in-text disclaimer separating paperwork from pedagogy, which told the rater the
answer and tempted only toward a spurious **2**. The whole reason these probes exist is the
**0 vs −1** boundary. GM-F's replacement tests both wrong directions at once and is the
stronger of the two probes.

Ops caught one issue in GM-F's replacement before wiring it into code: the passage read
"reworking the **P2** materials," where "P2" was meant as the grade level (P2 Form Teacher is
a real CIEPSS actor) but is also the ID of a different proposition in our rubric. A judge
model could answer "P2" by matching the literal token rather than reasoning to P4, which
would corrupt the diagnostic rather than sharpen it. Mark ruled: reword to drop the token
collision, keep everything else (including the diagnostic intent) unchanged. Changed
"P2 materials" → "Primary 2 materials" below — spelled out, no longer matches the P1–P5
ID pattern a judge would be scanning for.

**Design principle for any future probe items:** a good 0-item is *on the proposition's
topic* but silent on the specific thing the proposition asserts. An item that is merely
off-topic is easy and teaches us little. Items 11 and 12 now cover both patterns — 11 is
on-topic-but-silent, 12 is off-topic-but-adjacent — which is a deliberate pairing, not
redundancy.

- **Item 11 (probe — targets P4, on-topic but scope-silent):** "We have been reworking the
  Primary 2 materials this term. Some of it is mine, some came out of the level meeting. It is
  ongoing — we will see how it settles by the end of the year."
  - *Expected: P4 = 0.* **This is the item that does the real diagnostic work.** It is
    squarely on P4's topic — a teacher adapting curriculum materials — but states nothing
    about the *scope* of the adaptation or whether it meshes with or departs from MOE
    policy, which is the entire content of P4. Both wrong answers are informative: a judge
    scoring **2** has assumed the adaptation is small and compliant; a judge scoring **−1**
    has assumed it is a departure. Either way it invented the evidence P4 turns on. That
    is the failure mode most likely to corrupt real trials, where transcripts routinely
    touch a topic without settling the question.
- **Item 12 (probe — targets P1, vocabulary-adjacent absence):** "Our school is fairly small,
  so a lot of decisions just happen over lunch in the staff room rather than through any
  formal process — who is covering recess duty, whether we swap classrooms for an
  activity, that sort of thing."
  - *Expected: P1 = 0.* **Sound as drafted — GM-F confirmed, no change.** It carries genuine
    P1 bait: "decisions… rather than through any formal process" reads like authority sitting
    with individuals rather than the school, which is close to a P1 contradiction. But P1 is
    specifically about *authority for innovation*, and recess duty and classroom swaps are
    operational logistics. 0 is therefore the only defensible score, and a judge scoring **−1**
    has matched on the shape of the sentence rather than reading what the decisions are
    actually about.

**Scoring note:** these two are recorded separately from the 8/10 gate — track per-judge
whether each is scored 0 (correct), 2, or −1 (both incorrect, and diagnostically distinct:
scoring 2 suggests the judge over-reads adjacency as instantiation; scoring −1 suggests it
treats absence as contradiction, the exact failure §3.3's rubric warns against).
